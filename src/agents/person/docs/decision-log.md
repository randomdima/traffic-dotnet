# Decision log — the walker

Why this slice reads the way it does. Only decisions still binding are here: a superseded one is deleted,
not annotated. The rules themselves are [requirements.md](requirements.md); how a type works is its own
XML docs.

## 2026-09-07 — the walker's action set is the code's, and `PER-2` is retired

`PER-2` listed turning, walking, idling and entering a container — the four methods, restated where they
could drift from the type that has them. What each one costs and is bounded by is `PER-3` and the
containment rules; the list itself was cited by nothing.

## 2026-09-05 — the patience buys a road, and a car standing on the band is not one

A car standing on a zebra is a stretch of the *lane* and writes nothing on the walk (TER-5c.1), so past
`KerbPatienceS` the escape granted the band whatever was on it and walkers shoved stopped cars down their
own lanes. The escape takes a road and never a body: a driver's road is a claim handed back by driving on,
and a body over the paint hands nothing back. What tells them apart is the bar the pavement already holds a
body to (PER-24) — one coming through the band is traffic, one going nowhere on it is a standstill. The
clock had to be let run, since `HeldAtTheKerb` froze it on the true ground that a red ends and a gap
arrives, which a parked car does neither of. The rescue exemption is one instance of this rather than its
own argument. Walks given up went from 533 to 743, which is the honest price: a walker that cannot get past
a body has one rung where a driver walks ten. That is AGT-7's gap, priced.

## 2026-09-05 — a claim has a width, and the step round is an ask rather than an aim

A carriageway lane holds one car abreast and a claim on it is honestly one interval of arclength; a walking
lane is two bodies wide on purpose, and the same interval makes a single file of it. Everything walker-only
had grown to work around that hole and none of it could work, because the step turned off the line while
the grant stayed on it. A claim now carries the span its holder covers across the way, and whoever reads it
says where across the way *it* is (TER-4c.2) — read off the holder alone, a way was two-dimensional for
whoever was written into it and one-dimensional for everybody reading it. The step is that ask and not a
re-aim (PER-24): one offset, written once, read by the ask, the grant and the feet. `IsInTheWay` and
`PassM` went with it. Over five minutes of Odesa, 446 walks arrived against 433 and the longest hold fell
from 208 s to 121 s.

## 2026-09-05 — the walker has an exam, and the map it is asked on has no traffic

What a walker does was only ever asked of a city, where a count says the town is jammed and never which
shape of ground jammed it. `Footway` is the driving exam's lattice with one walk staged at each cell.
Nothing drives on it: half of what a walker owes the road is what the road owes it back, and a card that
failed with a car on the map leaves nobody able to say which agent was wrong. The first run named the crowd
fault exactly — every card passed but the two where somebody is *standing* in the way. A leg running out is
not a walk given up: a line is laid again from wherever the body has got to (PER-8), so only arriving tells
a body standing still from one about to walk on.

## 2026-09-04 — a walker off every line was granted the whole town

The pavement's grant is taken along a way, so a walker on no way was handed back the permission it started
the pass with, which is everything — a body with nothing to be cut by was a body nothing had cut. That
state is the town's commonest: a walk was not counted as on a way until it had walked a point of its own
line. Two faults, two answers. A walker standing at the first point of a line it has not walked yet *is* on
that way, fixed where it was wrong. What is genuinely off every way is asked of the ground — the ways under
the box swept to where it is going — as a permission and not a distance, because a body off every line has
no metre to measure one along. Bodies bind it and reaches do not, or every walker in a crowd holds the
metres in front of it. Nothing holds a body off the place it is walking to, or a paramedic is stranded a
body's width short of its casualty. The clock reads a standstill off the holder now, since two bodies off
every way can hold each other; left out, Odesa ran thirteen rings of walkers each waiting on the next.

## 2026-09-04 — the walk is cut at every body, and the step is what it does about one

The claims were right and the picture was right; the *permission* taken off them was computed a second time
from a scope that left a body going nowhere out. Every body in front now cuts the walk whatever it is
doing, since a grant reaching past one is a walker crossing ground it was refused (TER-4c.3); what the
body's movement decides is the reply and never the cut. Being held by the claims used to return a walker
out of its decision entirely — right for a queue, whose ground ends itself — leaving it with no clock and
no leg given up; fixing that alone cleared four recovery runs and a rescue. And a body standing where the
walk is *going* is not one to get past: PER-24 said so and the step tested for it, but the grant did not,
so a paramedic was walked off its own errand.

## 2026-09-03 — going nowhere is a speed and not a use

The walk was cut at a use rather than at a body. Which of the two ways a stretch is written down says how it
was *measured* — from a line with a margin, or from a pose at the body's true extent — and never whether
the body is moving; a hand at the wheel, a shove and a slide are all written from the pose and can all be
doing 50 km/h. The walker asks the body and not the row. "Coming through" written as *moving at all* was
right about the car and wrong about the walkers, and the town's crowds came back; the bar is what the feet
can get past, which PER-24 already said as *under way along the same lane*, the same figure the kerb holds
a rescue to (PER-15). Separately, the pavement's aside-of-the-line bar was **nought** (TER-4c.2), so a car
parked alongside a footway was one the walk went straight through; it is half a body now. The driving side
needed nothing: a driver's swerve costs an obstruction wait first, and a walker has none.

## 2026-08-30 — a walker walks against its stop, and a stopped rescue is not a rescue

The biggest heap was ten walkers at 1.8 m spacing each granted −0.20 m: the gap is 2.0 m and a walker's
stopping distance is 0.20 m, so every body had set off at full pace on a grant of a centimetre and come to
rest one stop inside the ground in front. Feet have no reverse and a held walker takes no decision, so the
column moved one stop at a time in lock step. The bar is now this tick's stride and the stop after it — not
the pace's own stopping distance, which was tried and made rings of two, because the creep out of a
violated gap is the only thing that breaks one. Separately, a fifty-two-second hold was a walker refused
the band by an ambulance standing still on it at 0.4 mm/s: PER-15's escape is disabled against a rescue
because a call lasts seconds and passes, so it now asks whether the rescue is coming through at all against
the walker's own pace. Zero was the wrong bar — a car creeping in a queue read as coming through.

## 2026-08-29 — the crossing patience is spent where the body is, not where its line still goes

Every crowd in twenty-five minutes of Odesa had at its head a body standing in the carriageway, refused the
band, with a patience clock reading zero. `AtTheKerb` tested the *line*: a walk laid onto a crossing is
consumed as the body walks it, so a walker part way over has no crossing point ahead and read as not
crossing anything. The escape could never arm for the one body that needs it. Where the body is standing
decides it now, which is what the drivable branch below it already did. Walks arrived went from 1868 to
2010.
