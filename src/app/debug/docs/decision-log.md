# Decision log — the debug layers

Why this slice reads the way it does. Only decisions still binding are here: a superseded one is deleted,
not annotated. The rules themselves are [requirements.md](requirements.md); how a type works is its own
XML docs.

## 2026-09-04 — the wash on a block is the strength of the claim

Every stretch used to be drawn at one wash, on the argument that a claim ahead and a committed one are the same
body's ground and a second shade would read as a different **kind** of ground. It held for the pieces of one
body's hold, and it cost the layer the reading it is opened at a junction for: half a dozen asks lying over
each other, all at one strength, with nothing on screen to say which of them their holders would give up.
The ladder that settles them ([`ClaimPriority`](../../../world/road/ClaimPriority.cs)) was the one property
of a claim the picture did not carry.

So the colour says whose and the wash says how strong, half down to a tenth across the ladder, taken off the
rung's own number so a level added between two costs the layer nothing. What the bar across a stretch's ends
says is unchanged and is now the only thing separating pieces of one hold, which is what it was always doing
— the pieces of one hold share a strength and therefore a wash.

## 2026-09-03 — the nodes layer draws every lane whole, and every mitre the town lays

The layer used to draw a lane between the stations a route joins and leaves it at, and to skip the mitre for
turning round on the spot. Both hid ground either network can hold a claim on: a walker standing at a node
had blocks reaching a band and a half past where the chevrons stopped, and a fan of them over mitres that
were drawn nowhere at all. A block with no line under it cannot be read — the block is then the only thing
on screen, and what it says about where its holder stands cannot be checked against anything.

So the layer draws every lane of both networks over the whole of its own line and every mitre the town lays,
with no filter. That is only affordable because the two producers were fixed to match: turning round lays no
mitre, and a lane is now cut down to exactly the ground it is walked over
([world/road](../../../world/road/docs/decision-log.md)). Drawing whole therefore draws no spur — what the
layer shows is what can be claimed, way for way.
